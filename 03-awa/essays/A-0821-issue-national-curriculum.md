## 고쳐 쓴 예시 — 가장 약한 문단(이유 1+2 통합)을 4.5+ 수준으로

> The most immediate benefit of a single national curriculum is the concentration of finite resources. Designing a rigorous course of study is expensive: it requires subject-matter experts, field testing, teacher training, and periodic revision. When a country maintains a dozen competing curricula, each of those costs is duplicated, and every version receives only a fraction of the available expertise. South Korea's national curriculum illustrates the alternative. Because a single framework governs all schools through the twelfth grade, the Ministry of Education can commission one panel of specialists to revise the mathematics sequence, pilot it nationwide, and retrain teachers on a single standard. The contrast with the United States before the Common Core initiative is instructive: individual states wrote their own standards, and a student judged "proficient" in one state could be two grade levels behind a proficient student in another. Consolidation does not merely save money; it buys depth with the money saved.

**무엇이 달라졌나**: ① 두 개의 얇은 이유를 하나의 두꺼운 이유로 통합 ② 고유명사 2개(한국 교육부, Common Core) ③ 문장 길이가 짧-김-짧으로 변주 ④ 마지막 문장이 단순 요약이 아니라 논증을 한 단계 밀어올림.

## 빠진 반론 문단 (모범 예시)

> The strongest objection to a fixed curriculum is that uniformity purchases efficiency at the price of responsiveness. A single syllabus written in the capital cannot anticipate the needs of a rural district where half the students will enter agriculture, or of an immigrant neighborhood where language instruction must come first. Worse, centralization concentrates risk: when one committee controls what every child learns, a single ideological capture — as in the recurring battles over Texas textbook adoption, whose decisions long shaped publishers' content far beyond that state — distorts an entire generation's education rather than one district's. This objection has force, but it argues for the right *scope* of a national curriculum, not against having one. A defensible system fixes a common core — literacy, mathematics, scientific reasoning, civics — while reserving a meaningful share of instructional hours for local and elective content. The goal is a floor beneath which no student falls, not a ceiling above which no school may rise.
